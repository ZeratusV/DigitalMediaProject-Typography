What is Typography

Typography is the style and appearance of text, and the art of working with it: how it is designed. Century Gothic, for example, is used in grade school because its simple letter shapes help children recognize an "a" and not a "a".

Learn More

Types of Typography

Serif and sans serif are the two main types.

Serif

Letterforms with small strokes at the ends.

Sans Serif

Styles without serifs.

Script

Type that imitates handwriting.

Fancy

Decorative type that stands out.

Typography Hierarchy

Typographic hierarchy is a system that organizes type in order of importance. It helps reading and is an important technique for communicating content effectively.

Typographical Concepts

Leading

The space between lines of text (paragraph spacing). It affects legibility.

Tracking

Uniform space between characters. T r a c k i n g

Kerning

The proportionality of the spaces between characters, and the process of adjusting it.

Typeface vs Font

They are not the same thing.

  • Typeface (Latin typus, "impression", and facies, "form"): a particular design of type, the collective name of a family of related fonts.
  • Font (French fonte, "casting"): a set of type of one face and size, covering the weights, widths and styles of a typeface.
  • Type foundries cast characters in lead alloys called fonts. The word was first used in 1668. The advent of digital made the distinction erroneous.

Typography Hierarchy
  • Font weight: the thickness, such as bold
  • Typeface: makes an element stand out from the rest
  • Font scale: the size of the text
  • Color
  • Layout: arrangement or placement gives prominence to certain elements, like a business card (today often just a name and a QR code)
